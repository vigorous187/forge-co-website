## Why Q4 2026 is the quarter to plan now

October through December is when GTA restaurants sell Thanksgiving tables, Diwali family dinners, Halloween nights, office parties, gift cards, and New Year's Eve seatings. This calendar replaces the old July–September plan. Use it from the week of September 22 onward, and confirm any parade or market date on the organizer's site before you print it on a menu.

Statutory dates below follow the Government of Canada's 2026 public-holiday list. Civic events move. Your Google Business Profile hours should match the dining room, not last year's poster.

If local visibility is already thin, book a [growth audit](/growth-audit/) in early October so photo, menu, and hours fixes land before holiday search traffic peaks.

## October content for GTA restaurants

October opens on a Thursday. The day before, Wednesday, September 30, is the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation. Treat that day as an observance: publish accurate hours, and do not hang a discount on it. Start the fall campaign on October 1.

**October 1–11: move guests indoors**

Patio season is over for most rooms. Post the dishes you want people to book in cool weather: braises, noodle soups, roast plates, and the drink you can actually keep in stock. Downtown spots can plan a late supper around Nuit Blanche. The all-night art event is usually the first Saturday of October, which is October 3 in 2026. Check the City of Toronto listing before you promise a 2 a.m. kitchen.

- **Social:** one hero fall dish, one "we're indoors now" photo of the room with lights on, one staff note about what to book for a weeknight
- **Email:** a short note to summer regulars with two October reservation windows, not a novel
- **Google Business Profile:** swap the cover photo off the patio, post fall hours, and add three current food photos

**Thanksgiving: Monday, October 12**

The long weekend is Saturday, October 10 through Monday, October 12. Publish the sitting times, the prix-fixe or family-style offer, and whether you take walk-ins. Many guests want a restaurant table so they are not hosting. Others want takeout sides and a pie with a pickup window.

Set holiday hours on Google before October 1. A Maps listing that still says "open regular hours" on Thanksgiving Monday sends people to a locked door. Our [search services](/services/search-forge/) cover that listing work when you do not have time to do it between prep lists.

Neighbourhood angle: residential strips (Leslieville, Bloor West, the Kingsway, Unionville, Port Credit) lean family and early. King West and the Entertainment District lean Sunday dinner and out-of-town visitors. Write the caption for the room you actually have.

**October 13–30: weeknights, then Halloween**

Leafs and Raptors home games are ordinary October traffic, not a campaign you should bet the month on. If a game dumps a crowd on your block, post a walk-in or late-kitchen note that afternoon. If it does not, ignore it.

Halloween is Saturday, October 31. Run two services on purpose.

- Early seating for families before street trick-or-treating. State the last seating for kids' meals.
- A later adult seating. Say whether costumes are welcome, whether masks come off at the table, and whether the kitchen closes at 10 or midnight.
- A single featured drink or dessert is enough. A full themed menu that the line cannot cook is how Halloween service falls apart.

Residential corners can post "eat first, then walk the block." Bar-forward rooms can post the late seating and the coat/bag situation. Skip generic pumpkin clip art.

## November content for GTA restaurants

November is booking season. The posts that pay for themselves are the ones that fill a private table or sell a gift card, not another latte photo.

**Diwali: Sunday, November 8**

Diwali (Lakshmi Puja) falls on Sunday, November 8, 2026. The surrounding days matter too: many families are gathering from that weekend through the early part of the next week. If you cook sweets, vegetarian thalis, grill platters, or any menu that works for a large family table, say so in the first line of the post. Include how many you can seat together and whether you take a deposit.

This is a GTA-wide family-dining weekend, especially where multi-generational bookings are already normal. Write the offer for the menu you cook and the neighbourhood you actually serve.

**Remembrance Day: Wednesday, November 11**

Keep the tone plain. Update hours. If you open, note it. If you close, say so by November 9. A moment of quiet at 11 a.m. does not need a graphic, a discount code, or a pun. Staff posts, if you make them, should be about the people on your team, not the logo.

**Parties, parades, and markets**

Corporate holiday parties get booked in November, not on December 20. Post the set menu, the buyout minimum, and the date you stop taking December holds. A real PDF or a four-line email beats a vague "now booking parties" story.

Use these as planning hooks only after you confirm the 2026 announcement:

- The Toronto Santa Claus Parade has often been the third Sunday in November. In 2026 that Sunday is November 15. Downtown and parade-route rooms can offer early brunch and a simple kids' plate. Do not print the date on a poster until the parade confirms it.
- The Royal Agricultural Winter Fair is usually at Exhibition Place in November. West-end restaurants can offer a pre-fair dinner once the fair publishes dates.
- The Distillery District holiday market and the Cavalcade of Lights at Nathan Phillips Square usually land in November. Nearby rooms should post late seating and a clear "no, we are not inside the market" line so guests are not surprised.

**Gift cards: November 16–30**

Canadian Thanksgiving is already over. Thursday, November 26 is American Thanksgiving, and Friday, November 27 is Black Friday. For a GTA dining room the useful move is gift cards and party deposits, not a second turkey weekend.

- Put the gift card link in the Instagram bio, the email footer, and a Google post.
- Say where cards can be redeemed (dine-in, takeout, merchandise) and when they expire. An expired-card argument in January is worse than no campaign.
- Photograph the physical card on a table in your room. Stock photos of wrapped boxes do not tell anyone the card is yours.

## December content for GTA restaurants

December content is logistics. Guests need the hours, the price, and how to get home.

**December 1–23: the party month**

Hanukkah begins at sundown on Friday, December 4, 2026. The first full day is Saturday, December 5, and the holiday runs through Saturday, December 12. If latkes, brisket, sufganiyot, or a family-style menu are actually on your board, post the dates and the reservation link. If they are not, do not costume the brand for a week.

Through December 23, rotate three assets:

1. The set menu and what it costs per person
2. One private-dining or buyout photo of your real room
3. Gift cards, again, aimed at people who still need a host gift

Office-tower lunch spots should post shortened December hours before the first quiet Friday. Neighbourhood rooms should post the opposite: weeknight tables while downtown empties out.

**Christmas week**

Christmas Eve is Thursday, December 24. Christmas Day is Friday, December 25. Boxing Day is Saturday, December 26. Decide, then publish, these four facts:

- Which of the three days you are open, and the exact last seating
- Whether the menu is the regular card, a prix fixe, or takeout only
- The deposit and cancellation rule
- Transit and parking, in one sentence

Boxing Day is a Saturday. Mall-adjacent and suburban rooms often see family groups who do not want to cook. Downtown rooms may be quiet. Plan labour from your own past Boxing Days, not from a generic holiday meme.

**December 27–31: staycations and New Year's Eve**

The week between Christmas and New Year's is staycation and visitor traffic. Post open tables for December 27–30. People book late because their own plans moved.

New Year's Eve is Thursday, December 31. Run it like a ticketed night even if you never use the word ticket.

- Early seating and late seating, with last call written down
- The price, the deposit, and what happens if someone cancels on December 30
- A note on how guests get home (subway, GO, rideshare pickup spot)
- A kitchen-close time you will actually keep

On Friday, January 1, either post reduced hours or post that you are closed. Silence reads as "open" on Google.

If you want the holiday traffic to show up on Maps and not only on Instagram, pair this calendar with the [Google Maps guide for Toronto restaurants](/blog/toronto-restaurants-google-maps-2026/). Hours and photos do more on December 24 than a new caption style.

**How to fill the grid:** on Sunday, write the week's three captions and set the Google post. Use photos you already have. An October 2 photo of the braise still works on October 20 if the caption is about a weeknight table, not "new menu launch."

## How to run this without a social team

One person can run this calendar in about an hour on Sunday plus ten minutes on the mornings when hours change.

Batch captions in a notes doc: dish, offer, date, link, and the one fact a guest needs (price, last seating, or deposit). Schedule the posts. Do not invent a new shoot for every week.

When a storm, a playoff series, or a parade date changes, replace the row. Do not keep a Halloween late-night post up if you decided to close the kitchen at 9.

Track three outcomes in a single note: reservation calls, gift card sales, and direction requests from Google. Repeat the post style that matched a full book. Drop the one that only collected likes.

The point of a Q4 calendar is a full room on the nights you meant to fill. Start with Thanksgiving hours and the gift card link. Those two are worth more than a perfect grid.
